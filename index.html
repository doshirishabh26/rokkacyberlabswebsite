Tour Payment System
✦ New System

How Tour Billing Works — Rolling Weekly ACH Instalments

Tour jobs use a completely different billing model than events. Instead of one upfront charge, the platform creates a separate weekly commitment per crew member per work week, anchored to each person's individual start date on the tour.

  • Anchor day = max(tour start date, position start date, crew hire date). Each crew member's weekly billing clock starts from their anchor.
  • Week 1 commitment — created the moment a crew member accepts an offer. ACH-pulled when within 7 days of the funding due date; charged to card immediately if the crew is hired inside that window.
  • Week 2 — auto-created on the day the tour starts. Same ACH / card-escalation rules apply.
  • Week 3, 4, 5… — created automatically by a daily cron only on the crew member's anchor-day anniversary (every 7 days from tour start), preventing premature commitment creation.
  • Funding due date = anchor + (week − 1) × 7 days — the start of that work week. ACH batch fires on or before this date.
  • Crew payout eligible = anchor + week × 7 + 7 days — 7 days after the work week ends, paid out independently, no need to wait for job completion.
  • Mid-tour hires start their own weekly clock. A crew member who joins week 3 of a tour gets Week 1 committed at hire, not "Week 3" of the tour.
  • If no card is on file when a hire happens inside the T-7 window, the hire is automatically rolled back and the company is notified.
  • T&A has no effect on tour commitment amounts. Weekly pay is a flat contract — OT and attendance discrepancies do not change the weekly charge.

Once accepted, the offer immediately creates a Week 1 commitment and queues it for the next ACH batch — no further action needed from the company.

Batch Timing

When Does the Money Actually Move?

  • ACH pull — fires when a commitment's funding due date is within 7 days
  • Card charge — fires immediately if the hire happens inside the 7-day window
  • Settlement — ACH typically settles in 2–4 business days; funds move to Stripe balance
  • Crew payout — 7 days after the work week ends, Stripe Connect transfer fires to crew's bank account automatically
  • All of this is fully automated — companies set it up once and the platform handles every week

✦ New

Hotel Provided for Crew — Set at Job Creation, Affects OT Calculations

A new "Hotel provided for crew" checkbox has been added to the Additional Details section of the job creation wizard (alongside Require US Passport, Dress Code, and Tags).

  • Signals to crew that accommodation is covered for out-of-town talent — shown prominently on the job listing to attract a wider radius of applicants
  • When checked, the short-turnaround rest threshold drops from 8 hours to 6 hours — crew earn overtime sooner on back-to-back show days because hotel guests are expected to commute faster
  • The setting flows through to the Time & Attendance tab automatically — no separate toggle needed on each attendance day
  • PMs can still override the hotel flag per individual crew member on the attendance tab if a specific person is commuting locally

Example: Jenny works Mon night until 2 AM, then is called back at 8 AM Tuesday. Without hotel: 6-hour gap > 8h threshold → no OT carryover. With hotel checked: 6-hour gap = exactly 6h threshold → short-turnaround kicks in → Tuesday OT clock starts earlier.

At this point Jenny is 2h 30m into work block 2 (7:00 PM → 9:30 PM). The 5-hour continuous work rule requires a proper 1-hour walk-away meal break before the 5-hour mark. That deadline is midnight (7:00 PM + 5h = 12:00 AM).

⚠ 2 Meal Violations — Why They Happened

1
First meal break — 45 minutes late. After the 7:00 PM break-in, Jenny worked continuously until 12:45 AM — 5 hours 45 minutes. Labor rules require a proper 1-hour walk-away break within 5 hours of continuous work (deadline: midnight). The company provided a meal at 9:30 PM but Jenny ate on-site while working. A Provided Meal does not satisfy this requirement — only a logged Break out → Break in pair counts. Result: 1 penalty at 1.5× hourly rate.
2
Second meal break — 45 minutes late. A second meal is required once total billable hours cross 10 hours. Jenny crossed the 10-hour mark at approximately 2:45 AM. The second provided meal was logged at 3:30 AM — 45 minutes after the threshold. Again, "provided meal" does not stop the clock, so the 10h mark passed with no proper break. Result: 1 penalty at 1.5× hourly rate.

When to use manual correction: If a crew member forgot to scan in or out, or scanned at the wrong time, the PM can add or remove any scan event with a free-text reason. Every correction is logged in a permanent edit history so the record is always auditable. Scans that were added manually are labelled "Manual" in the timeline (visible in all examples above).

All 5 scan types explained:
• Site in — crew arrives on site; billable clock starts.
• Break out — crew leaves the worksite for a break; clock pauses.
• Break in — crew returns from break; clock resumes.
• Provided meal — company catering delivered on-site. Clock does NOT stop. Records the company's obligation being met, but crew remains active. Does not satisfy labor-rule meal break requirements without an accompanying Break out/in.
• Site out — crew departs; billable clock stops for the day.
Invoicing & Reconciliation
✦ New

Finalization Auto-Creates Invoices

When a PM finalizes attendance, the platform automatically creates a supplemental invoice for each crew member based on their actual vs. scheduled hours:

  • Positive delta → submitted invoice, charged to company via ACH/card
  • Negative delta → credit invoice, becomes company credit balance
  • Zero delta or no-show → no invoice created
✦ New

Company Credit Ledger — Auto-Applies to Next Invoices

When a crew member works fewer hours than committed, a credit invoice is auto-generated. The credit balance sits in the company's ledger and is automatically applied to the next invoice on the same job — oldest credit consumed first (FIFO).

  • Credit is applied when you click "Apply Credit" on any pending invoice — no manual math needed
  • Crew payout is always the full invoice subtotal — only the company charge is reduced
  • If the credit fully covers an invoice, the company is charged $0.00
  • Remaining unused credit carries forward to the next invoice cycle
✦ New

Labor Reconciliation in Payments Tab

The Payments tab now has a Labor Reconciliation section showing committed pay vs. actual pay from confirmed attendance, with OT and DT dollars surfaced per crew member.

✦ New

Request Refund to Bank — Company & Admin Flow

If unused credit remains after all invoices on a job are settled, the company can request a cash refund to their bank account. The request is reviewed and processed by a Show Crew Network admin:

  • Company sees the credit balance on their Invoices tab with a refund request option
  • Admin portal shows a "Refund to Bank" button on the job's Invoices & Credits section
  • Admin processes the refund directly — no Stripe dashboard access required
  • Full audit trail: every credit creation, application, and refund is logged
